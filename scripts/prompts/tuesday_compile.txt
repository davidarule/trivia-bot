COVERAGE
- Mix of Australian and major global stories, weighted roughly 60/40 toward Australian content.
- Skew toward stories from days 3-10 ago over the last 24 hours, since trivia hosts usually write questions a few days ahead.

STORY TYPES TO INCLUDE
- Celebrity, royals, entertainment news
- Sport results, oddities, records, retirements, transfers, milestones
- Major business or sport news (acquisitions, launches) with a clean named answer
- Politics, court rulings, political gaffes, resignations with a clean named answer; exclude grim ones
- Viral animals, weird science, quirky human-interest, unusual records, weather oddities
- Food, drink, pop culture milestones
- Australian local quirks: wildlife incidents, weather oddities, viral local news

STORY TYPES TO EXCLUDE
- Deaths, fatal accidents, murders, terror attacks
- War, conflict, military casualties
- Natural disasters with significant casualties
- Sexual assault, child abuse, domestic violence
- Serious illness of public figures (unless the story is the recovery/return)
- Cold cases, missing persons, historical disappearances, presumed-murdered investigations, body recoveries — excluded even if framed as a "breakthrough" or "development"

The EXCLUDE list is mandatory and overrides every INCLUDE pattern, including "Australian local quirks". If a story belongs in both categories, exclude it.

QUESTION PATTERNS TO ACTIVELY HUNT FOR
- "Who has the new #1 ___?" (charts: Billboard Hot 100, ARIA, box office, Steam, NYT bestsellers)
- "Who won ___?" (sporting events, awards, elections, talent-show finales)
- "Which league / company / competition is in the news?"
- "World record set or broken?"
- "What unusual thing happened in [Australian city]?"
- "Who said / did / wore ___?"
- "What's new / launched / announced?"

If a pattern's first search doesn't surface a clearly notable story, try at least one alternative angle (named sponsors, specific geographies, common acronyms) before concluding the pattern is quiet this week.

COMPILATION RULES
- Deduplicate stories that appeared across multiple daily intakes
- Drop stale stories that have been overtaken by newer developments
- Promote stories that have grown in significance since first intake
- Add anything from the fresh Tuesday sweep that wasn't in the daily intakes

OUTPUT FORMAT
Sections in this order:

SECTION 1 — News brief (always)
Grouped under five headings: Australia, Global, Sport, Entertainment, Weird/Quirky. Under each, 3-5 bullets. Each bullet: one sentence stating the fact, one line of context (date, location, or why it matters). Aim for 12-20 bullets total.

SECTION 2 — Most likely trivia question shortlist (always)
3-5 stories shaped as the actual question the host might ask, each followed by the clean answer.

SECTION 3 — Celebrity birthdays today (always)
10 A-list household-name celebrities born on this calendar date (any year), each with their age today. Skip anyone who isn't a genuine household name globally or in Australia — no reality TV contestants, minor YouTubers, or niche figures. If fewer than 10 genuine A-listers were born today, list however many qualify and note the count.

SECTION 4 — This month in history

SOURCES
- Australian: ABC News Australia, news.com.au, The Guardian Australia, Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, 9News, 7News, Newcastle Herald
- Global: BBC, Reuters, AP, CNN, Guardian
- Specialist: Billboard, ARIA, Box Office Mojo, ESPN/Sky Sports
- Avoid tabloids that fabricate
- Verify any surprising claim against a second source
