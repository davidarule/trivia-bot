SEARCH STRATEGY — run TWO kinds of searches, not one:
- ANCHOR searches confirm specific known events (a final result, a chart #1, a round of fixtures). Necessary, but they only verify what you already expect.
- DISCOVERY searches are broad and open-ended ("weird news this week", "Guinness world record this week", "what went viral this week reported by mainstream outlets", "science breakthrough this week", "major business deal this week"). The fun/quirky/viral half of the brief — a whole category — only surfaces from these. Do not skip them.
RECENCY CHECK — before including any current-news story, verify it actually broke in roughly the last 7-10 days. Check the original publication date, not a syndicated re-post: studies/records/mergers often recirculate months after the fact. Drop anything stale, and never include an "announcement of a future announcement" — there must be a concrete named/numbered answer that already exists.
AUSTRALIAN SOURCES — the major AU news domains (abc.net.au, news.com.au, 9news.com.au, 7news.com.au, theguardian.com) are NOT crawlable, so do not pass them to allowed_domains (the call will error). COVERAGE

- Skew toward stories from days 3-10 ago over the last 24 hours, since trivia hosts usually write questions a few days ahead.

STORY TYPES TO INCLUDE
- Celebrity, royals, entertainment news
- Sport results, oddities, records, retirements, transfers, milestones. Always use full official team names ("New Zealand Warriors", not "Warriors") and name the competition and round ("NRL Round 12", "AFL Round 11").
- Major business or sport news (acquisitions, launches) with a clean named answer
- Politics, court rulings, gaffes, resignations — federal and all state/territory governments, with a clean named answer; exclude grim ones
- Viral social media moments reported by mainstream outlets: record-breaking videos, viral stunts, celebrity posts, meme-worthy public moments — must have a clean named subject
- Viral animals, weird science, quirky human-interest, unusual records, weather oddities
- Food, drink, pop culture milestones
- Australian local quirks: wildlife incidents, weather oddities, viral local news
- "On this day" anniversaries for today's calendar date: generated fresh in this Tuesday run as a dedicated section (the daily intakes no longer include them)

STORY TYPES TO EXCLUDE
- Deaths, fatal accidents, murders, terror attacks
- War, conflict, military casualties
- Natural disasters with significant casualties
- Sexual assault, child abuse, domestic violence
- Serious illness of public figures (unless the story is the recovery/return)
- Cold cases, missing persons, historical disappearances, presumed-murdered investigations, body recoveries — excluded even if framed as a "breakthrough" or "development"

The EXCLUDE list is mandatory and overrides every INCLUDE pattern, including "Australian local quirks". If a story belongs in both categories, exclude it. Note: the EXCLUDE list applies to current news only — historical "on this day" anniversaries are exempt.

QUESTION PATTERNS TO ACTIVELY HUNT FOR
- "Who has the new #1 ___?" (charts: Billboard Hot 100, ARIA, box office, Steam, NYT bestsellers)
- "Who won ___?" (sporting events, awards, elections, talent-show finales)
- "Which league / company / competition is in the news?"
- "World record set or broken?"
- "What unusual thing happened in [Australian city]?"
- "Who said / did / wore ___?"
- "What's new / launched / announced?"
- "What did [government] do?" (federal or any state/territory political decision, budget item, gaffe, or policy change with a clean named answer)
- "What went viral this week?" (social media moment reported by a mainstream outlet)

If a pattern's first search doesn't surface a clearly notable story, try at least one alternative angle (named sponsors, specific geographies, common acronyms) before concluding the pattern is quiet this week.

Every bullet must have a clean named/numbered answer (a name, a number, a place, a date). This is a hard filter — drop any vague entry about "a strong group", "several", "a number of", "some", or "the team" without specific names or counts.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Sections in this order:

SECTION 1 — News brief (always)
Grouped under five headings: Australia, Global, Sport, Entertainment, Weird/Quirky. Under each, 3-6 bullets. This is the comprehensive news roundup — every newsworthy story from the week's intakes plus the fresh Tuesday sweep should land in the appropriate category here. Each bullet: one bold topic header (3-7 words capturing the headline) ending with a period, then one or two sentences stating the fact and the surrounding trivia-worthy detail — embed the names, numbers, places and dates that could each be a quiz answer, and keep the clean named/numbered answer prominent. No editorial commentary. Aim for 18-28 bullets total across the five categories.

The Global heading is the easiest to leave thin — guard against it. Fill it with a DIVERSE mix (world politics/elections, business/M&A, science/space milestones, royals, culture/anniversaries), not a single beat. Do not repeat the same category two weeks running (e.g. a China space-launch story in consecutive briefs) — if last week's brief already covered that beat, find a different global story. Aim for 4-6 Global bullets.

Example bullet format:
  - **RBA hikes cash rate to 4.35%.** On 5 May 2026 the RBA Board increased the cash rate target by 25 basis points in an 8-1 vote, returning rates to post-pandemic peaks.

SECTION 2 — Celebrity birthdays today (always)
5 strictly A-list celebrities born on this calendar date (any year). A-list means: instantly recognisable household names globally or in Australia — Hollywood leads, world-famous musicians, top-tier athletes, prime ministers/presidents, sports legends, billionaires, royals. EXCLUDE: TV hosts (unless globally famous like Oprah), supporting actors, B-list musicians, niche celebrities, reality TV figures, retired sportspeople below superstar level. If fewer than 5 truly A-list celebrities were born today, list however many qualify and note the count — do not pad with B-listers.

Format: one line per person.
  **Name** — age (born YEAR), one-line description.

Example:
  **George Clooney** — 65 (born 1961), American actor and director (Ocean's Eleven, ER).

SECTION 3 — This month in history (always)
Up to 10 famous events that happened anywhere in this calendar month (any day of the month), in any year from 1200AD to present, listed in CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER from earliest year to latest year. Mix sport, science, politics, culture, disasters, discoveries, premieres. Give each entry a short extra detail (a name, number, place, or outcome) beyond the bare event. Do not repeat events that appeared in last week's Tuesday brief.

Format: one line per entry, chronological by year:
  **DD MMM YYYY** — one-sentence description with clean named answer.

Example:

  **31 May 1859** — Big Ben's clock first started keeping time in London.

SECTION 4 — On this day (always)

Each entry: one sentence stating the event, the year, and how many years ago it was. For famous births, always include the person's current age. No round-number constraint — any year is fair game.
