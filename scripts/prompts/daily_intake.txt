Find light-hearted, trivia-worthy news stories from the past 48 hours suitable for a pub trivia night in Newcastle/Lake Macquarie, NSW, Australia. Today's date is [DATE]. This is a daily intake report that will be collated into a weekly trivia brief on Tuesday at 4pm AEST.

IMPORTANT: Begin your response directly with the first section heading. Do not include any preamble, search narration, or meta-commentary.

COVERAGE
- Mix of Australian and major global stories, weighted roughly 60/40 toward Australian content.
- Australian politics should cover federal AND all state/territory governments — not just NSW.

STORY TYPES TO INCLUDE
- Celebrity, royals, entertainment news
- Sport results, oddities, records, retirements, transfers, milestones. Always use full official team names ("New Zealand Warriors", not "Warriors") and name the competition and round ("NRL Round 12", "AFL Round 11").
- Major business or sport news (acquisitions, launches). Do not filter for "quirky" — a major company or league story is marquee trivia content even when serious in tone, as long as it has a clean named answer.
- Politics, court rulings, political gaffes, resignations — federal and all state/territory governments. Include any with a clean named answer; exclude only the grim ones (see below).
- Viral social media moments: TikTok, YouTube, or X/Instagram moments that have crossed into mainstream news coverage (must be reported by a major outlet, not just social shares).
- Viral animals, weird science, quirky human-interest, unusual records, weather oddities
- Food, drink, pop culture milestones
- Australian local quirks: wildlife incidents, weather oddities, viral local news. Australian hosts love these and they are often missed by global wires.

STORY TYPES TO EXCLUDE
- Deaths, fatal accidents, murders, terror attacks
- War, conflict, military casualties
- Natural disasters with significant casualties
- Sexual assault, child abuse, domestic violence
- Serious illness of public figures (unless the story is the recovery/return)
- Cold cases, missing persons, historical disappearances, presumed-murdered investigations, body recoveries — excluded even if framed as a "breakthrough" or "development"

The EXCLUDE list is mandatory and overrides every INCLUDE pattern.

SEARCH STRATEGY — run TWO kinds of searches, not one. ANCHOR searches confirm specific known events (a result, a chart #1). DISCOVERY searches are broad and open-ended ("weird news this week", "Guinness world record this week", "what went viral this week reported by mainstream outlets", "science breakthrough this week") — the fun/quirky/viral material only surfaces from these, so do not skip them. Note: the major AU news domains are NOT crawlable via allowed_domains; run `scripts/fetch_au_news.py` for recent AU headlines via RSS (ABC, Guardian AU, SMH, The Age, 7News, 9News, Newcastle Herald — news.com.au blocks fetches and is omitted), then open a story to confirm detail. Also use Wikipedia "2026 in Australia" and AAP/syndication.

QUESTION PATTERNS TO ACTIVELY HUNT FOR
Use these patterns to guide what to search for. They do NOT determine output structure — output is grouped by category (see OUTPUT FORMAT below).

- "Who has the new #1 ___?" — songs, albums, films, streaming shows, video games, books. Check Billboard Hot 100, ARIA Singles, weekend box office, Steam, NYT bestsellers. If the same artist or title tops multiple charts simultaneously, combine into a single bullet — do not list the same story twice under different chart names.
- "Who won ___?" — major sporting events, awards shows, elections, talent-show finales.
- "Which league / company / competition is in the news?" — any major event with a clean named answer.
- "World record set or broken?" — sport, science, Guinness-style achievements.
- "What unusual thing happened in [Australian city]?" — wildlife incidents, weather oddities, viral local news.
- "Who said / did / wore ___?" — quotable gaffes, viral moments, surprising public statements, notable red-carpet moments with a specific verifiable detail (time taken to make, designer, record broken). Exclude vague observations such as who arrived late, who looked good, or any entry without a clean named/numbered answer.
- "What's new / launched / announced?" — major product launches, scientific findings with a clean headline, surprise album drops, casting announcements.
- "What did [government] do?" — federal or any state/territory political decision, budget item, political gaffe or policy change with a clean named answer.
- "What went viral this week?" — social media moment reported by a mainstream outlet. Must have a clean named subject.

Each bullet must have a clean named/numbered answer (a name, a number, a place, a date). This is a hard filter — if you cannot identify a specific name, number, place or date as the answer, drop the bullet entirely. Vague entries about "a strong group", "several players", "a number of", "some", or "the team" without specific names or counts are not acceptable.

Examples of bullets to drop:
  Bad: "The Knights had a strong group of players selected for the NSW City vs Country campaign." — no names, no number.
  Bad: "Several Hollywood stars attended a charity gala in LA this weekend." — no names.
  Bad: "A new Australian film opened to good reviews." — no title, no numbers.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Begin directly with the first section heading. No title, no preamble, no meta-commentary.

Use these five section headings, in this order, formatted as markdown level-2 headings with the emoji shown:

## 🇦🇺 Australia
## 🌍 Global
## ⚽ Sport
## 🎬 Entertainment
## 🦊 Weird/Quirky

Under each heading, list bullets. Each bullet:
  - Bold topic header (3-7 words capturing the headline) ending with a period
  - One sentence stating the fact with the clean named/numbered answer
  - No second sentence, no editorial commentary, no "context:" lines

Example bullet format:
  - **Margot Robbie's 761-hour Met Gala gown.** Margot Robbie's custom Chanel dress for the 2026 Met Gala took 761 hours to make, with a "Fashion Is Art" theme.

Skip any section that has no items today — do not force entries into empty categories. Aim for 8-15 bullets total across all sections. Slow news day = fewer is fine.
